## Top 10: the backlog

| # | Item | Votes | What it means | Why the panel wanted it (evidence from the runs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Why did this change?" explainer** (I-02) | 16 | Break every headline number down into its causes. A price becomes base cost × demand × inventory × discounts; the wage becomes excess demand × stickiness; unemployment becomes private, public and self-employed. A "why?" link on each card. | Students couldn't tell whether +165% inflation came from wages, demand, inventory or measurement. Without causes, the model can't be taught. |
| 2 | **Trustworthy indicators** (I-03, I-04, I-21, I-31) | 14 (16 with the 2 votes for I-31, merged in; tied with #1, ranked second because the explainer will expose the measurement problems) | A chained monthly CPI with a warm-up base period and annualised rates. ILO-style labour statistics (private, public, self-employed, participation, discouraged workers). Real wage and the cost of a basket vs a month's wage. Debt sustainability (r vs g, interest burden) with the risk premium shown apart from the policy rate. | Unemployment flipped 0% ↔ 86% with public works. CPI read +165% to +417% with no money printed. The Taylor rule was pinned at 20% for six months. Bond rates of 33% were mostly the policy rate. |
| 3 | **Scenario comparison with locked seeds** (I-06, I-07) | 11 | Run A vs B on the same seed, side-by-side charts with divergence highlighted; saved configurations; multi-seed spread ("welfare beats baseline in 9 of 10 seeds"). | The lesson is the *difference* between policies, and single runs invite "your run was lucky". |
| 4 | **Role-play, turn-based mode** (I-08) | 10 | Teams with role-specific levers (central bank, finance ministry, bank, industry, union), monthly turns, hidden information, a debrief after each turn. | Turns the sandbox into a class activity; the panel saw it as the real onboarding. |
| 5 | **Instructor console and shock library** (I-23, I-24) | 9 | Pause, lock settings, inject shocks: drought, oil shock, pandemic, bank failure, destroyed capacity. Each with its expected textbook effect, which also serves as validation. | Instructors want to create the "case" moment; shocks with known effects also test the model. |
| 6 | **Policy-sensitivity audit and a healthy reference scenario** (I-12, I-33) | 7 | Make sure the levers move the outcomes. Provide a "healthy economy" reference. Add "why is this zero?" messages (mortgages, foreclosures, luck). | In-need ended at 73–91% across four very different policies. Mortgages were 0 in every run with no explanation. |
| 7 | **Labour participation collapse** (I-11) | 4 | Find out why labour offered falls 52 → 8–32 units a day; fix it, or show it (penalties, specialisation, autonomous production). | Participation fell 40–85% in every scenario; the dashboard showed 0% unemployment meanwhile. |
| 8 | **Guided missions and learn mode** (I-01, I-25) | 3 | Scenarios with a protagonist, a question and learning objectives. Plain-language tooltips and teaching notes on every metric and lever; advanced settings folded away. | Newcomers didn't know what to do or what a "unit" is. |
| 9 | **Auto-generated debrief** (I-10) | 2 | An event timeline ("day 92: central bank hit 20%; day 135: debt over the limit; …") plus the five key charts, ready for discussion. | Instructors don't have time to dig through the data. |
| 10 | **Model health page** (I-26) | 2 (+ Aoife's endorsement) | Automatic checks against stylized facts (Okun's law, Phillips-curve slope, consumption and wage shares of GDP), pass/fail shown to the instructor. | The model should flag its own implausible behaviour before a class. |
